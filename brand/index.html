Identity

Brand

The chronicle globe, the brandtype and the rules for using them.

The mark

Space above the line. The record below it.

The chronicle globe is divided at the equator. Above it is the map: meridians and a parallel, the Earth as space. Below it are ruled lines, like the entries in a chronicle: time, written down. One entry ends in a brass diamond, which marks one place at one date, on the record. The lines are ragged and unfinished on purpose. The record is open, and it grows by review.

Brandtype

A globe in the word, and a timeline beneath it.

CHRONICLE is set in Source Serif 4 at display optical size, in tracked capitals, the register of treaties, charters and gazetteers. Its O is the globe in miniature, with the map above the equator and one entry below. OF EARTH is set in Inter, widely tracked, and stands on a graduated timeline with a single brass date marker, the same ruler that runs under the animated globe on the front page. Every logotype is outlined, so none depends on installed fonts.

Colour

Ink, paper and one metal.

The palette is drawn from bound atlases and state papers. Brass is the only accent and marks one thing at a time. Layer colours exist for the map and stay out of large surfaces.

Usage

Rules.

  • The diamond is always brass; everything else in the mark is one ink colour.
  • Never rotate or flip the mark: the map is always above the equator, the record always below.
  • Never add markers, fill the globe, even out the ragged lines, or apply gradients or shadows.
  • Do not use the globe-O outside the wordmark, and never set the wordmark in live text.
  • Clear space on all sides equals twice the diamond's height. Minimum size: 24 px for the mark and 160 px wide for the stacked lockup; below 32 px, use the favicon cut (one meridian, three lines).
  • No flags, seals, crests or national symbols beside the mark. The atlas takes no side.
