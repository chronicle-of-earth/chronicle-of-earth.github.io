Five kinds of record

An entity supplies identity: a polity, territory, organisation or place. An event describes an occurrence. A claim states a proposition with a time and a scope — a capital, a membership, a population, control of a territory. Evidence relates a source to one event or claim. Geometry supplies a spatial representation without deciding which actor owns it.

Labels are finding aids, not names valid forever. Historical names are themselves dated claims. External identifiers and encyclopedia links support discovery; they never merge identities automatically.

A deliberately small vocabulary

Name, capital, control, claim, recognition, membership, population, existence, geometry, succession and government type. Adding a predicate means updating the schema, the semantic rules, the documentation and the tests — together.

Control, claims and recognition

These are separate predicates. A territory can have simultaneous control and claim records for different actors. Recognition is always recognition by a named actor, never universal recognition. Two competing claims remain two records, each with its own evidence. assertedBy identifies the claimant or the source’s viewpoint — never the editor.

Time, stated honestly

All dates are civil days in the proleptic Gregorian calendar, using astronomical year numbering, so the model already reaches before the common era. Original calendar expressions are retained beside the normalised date.

  • A date estimate has inclusive earliest and latest bounds and a precision label. Bounds express a range of possibilities, not a probability.
  • A state interval includes its start and excludes its end.
  • An end can be known, unknown, deliberately unbounded, or ongoing — observed in effect as of a stated date, and only possible after it.

For any date the atlas reports whether a record is certain, possible or outside its temporal bounds. That temporal match is separate from evidence confidence: “certain” means certainly within the stated time bounds, never certainty that the proposition is true.

Observations are not carried forward

Population and other measurements are point-in-time observations, with their method and measured geography. Nothing is interpolated or carried forward implicitly. A 1990 census does not describe 1995.

Sources and review

Discovery often starts with Wikipedia; facts are traced to authoritative evidence wherever it exists. An encyclopedia is a navigation layer, not a primary historical source. Every assertion needs supporting evidence, a temporal scope and a confidence rationale.

Promotion from draft requires a human reviewer to check sources, identity continuity, dates, uncertainty and reuse terms. Review labels are editorial metadata; repository permissions enforce who may promote. A release command refuses unreviewed drafts and synthetic fixtures outright.

Corrections and releases

Records are corrected, not silently overwritten: correction chains preserve what was published and why it changed. Releases are deterministic and content-addressed, with checksums and a manifest, so a shared view can be reproduced exactly.

Reference geography

Present-day boundaries, roads, rivers and relief help orientation. They are source-pinned and checksum-verified, and they are always labelled as present-day reference — not as historical borders, and not as a ruling on any dispute. Missing historical geometry is never filled with current borders without an explicit label.
