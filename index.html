Who governs, who claims, who recognises.

Principles

Six commitments the atlas will not trade away.

  1. Evidence before assertion

    No fact is displayed without supporting evidence, a temporal scope and a stated confidence rationale. Passing a schema check is not historical review.

  2. Control, claims and recognition stay separate

    Who governs a territory, who claims it and who recognises whom are distinct records. Recognition is always by a named actor.

  3. Disagreement is preserved

    Competing claims remain separate records with their own evidence. The atlas shows where sources disagree instead of choosing a winner out of sight.

  4. Change is discrete

    Borders do not morph. No intermediate frame claims a historical state that never existed.

  5. Missing is not absent

    Unknown is shown as unknown: never as zero, never as an empty map. Coverage gaps are published.

  6. Reference is not history

    Today's roads, names and boundaries appear only for orientation, always labelled as present-day reference.
